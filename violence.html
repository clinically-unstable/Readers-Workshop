VII

Circle VII: Violence

Violence against others, self, or God

The seventh circle is divided into three rings for violence against others, against oneself, and against God, nature, or human work.

Outer ring

Those violent toward others, including tyrants and murderers, are immersed in the boiling river of blood called the Phlegethon. Centaurs guard its banks.

Middle ring

Those who took their own lives become thorny trees, tormented by Harpies. The squanderers, who violently destroyed their possessions, are hunted through the wood.

Inner ring

Those violent toward God, nature, or art endure a desert of burning sand and falling fire. The groups include blasphemers, those Dante calls sodomites, and usurers.

The journey onward

The monster Geryon carries Dante and Virgil down from this circle toward Malebolge, the realm of fraud.

The guardian of the circle

The Minotaur guards the entrance to the ring of bloodshed. Virgil distracts the enraged creature so the travelers can descend to the river Phlegethon.
