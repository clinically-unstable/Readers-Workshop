Overview of Dante & the Beliefs of the Divine Comedy

Dante Alighieri

Divine Comedy

Dante Alighieri was an Italian writer and poet born on 1265, in Florence, Italy

At a time when Latin was the dominant language of literature, Dante broke tradition by writing in the Florence dialect

Furthermore, Divine Comedy helped establish a widespread standardized Italian language.

However, Dante was deeply controversial in his lifetime. He fiercly criticized the Catholic Church, and placed multiple popes in the Inferno (Hell) due to corrpution. Infact, many people mentioned in the Inferno were just historical figures that Dante disliked.

In 1302, Pope Boniface VIII banished him from Florence and threatened Dante with execution. In response, Boniface was put in the Eighth Circle of Hell.

The Divine Comedy is an epic poem written by Dante Alighieri, describing the journey through the three realms of the afterlife: Hell, Purgatory, and Heaven.
